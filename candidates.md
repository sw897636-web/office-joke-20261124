# Office joke candidates — scored against the house judge's five criteria

Criteria, most important first: (1) a real one-line joke about office life;
(2) funny — punchline surprises and pays off; (3) one line, setup + punchline
together, no rambling; (4) fresh — a specific office detail, not a tired gag;
(5) clear on first read, tight wording. Scores are out of 5 per criterion.

## A — the pick (revised, see main.ts)
> The office fridge has a yoghurt from 2019 with its own parking space, and nobody dares move it because by now it outranks us all.
- (1) 5 — a joke, not an observation: setup then punchline. (2) 5 — two beats:
  the yoghurt's "own parking space", then the seniority punchline. (3) 5 — one
  sentence. (4) 5 — communal-fridge archaeology is a specific, lived detail;
  nobody's heard this exact line. (5) 5 — plain words, instant read.

## B
> I spent an hour colour-coding a spreadsheet nobody will open, which is the closest thing to job security this office offers.
- (1) 5. (2) 4 — dry and neat, but wry rather than laugh-out-loud. (3) 5.
  (4) 4 — colour-coding spreadsheets is specific, though the "nobody reads my
  work" angle is familiar. (5) 5.

## C
> My calendar auto-declined a meeting because it overlapped with lunch, and for the first time ever I trusted the algorithm.
- (1) 5. (2) 3 — the punchline ("trusted the algorithm") is soft and lands
  late. (3) 5. (4) 4 — auto-decline is a real, specific detail. (5) 5.

## D
> Our office plants keep dying, so HR replaced them with a poster of a forest and called it a wellness initiative.
- (1) 4 — reads slightly more like satire than a joke with a punchline.
  (2) 4. (3) 5. (4) 4 — "wellness initiative" is nicely specific office
  jargon. (5) 5.

## E
> HR said our team is a family, so I asked who does the dishes and now I'm on a performance improvement plan.
- (1) 5. (2) 4 — the PIP twist is good. (3) 5. (4) 3 — "we're a family" is a
  well-worn office trope. (5) 5.

## F
> My boss called a meeting to discuss why we have too many meetings, and it ran twenty minutes over.
- (1) 5. (2) 3 — the "ran over" twist is pleasant but predictable. (3) 5.
  (4) 2 — meeting-about-meetings is a familiar premise. (5) 5.

## Why A wins
A is the only line that scores 5 on all five criteria: a genuine joke with a
surprising punchline, a single sentence, a fresh specific detail (the
communal fridge's ancient yoghurt), and wording anyone reads instantly.
Deliberately avoided: "this meeting could have been an email", Monday
mornings, and the copier/printer.
